Research

Evidence and evaluation from the RMD UK network — near-peer BLS education, instructor development, and outcomes.

Research across the network

Research at RMD UK

Research is carried out across different RMD UK sites. Some of the original key research into near-peer BLS teaching and assessment in UK Higher Education Institutions originated at RMD Birmingham, and work continues across the network into the pedagogy and technique of BLS training. This spans papers, reviews, and presentations, supported by regular cross-site meetings.

Publications & projects

Highlighted publications

RMD Birmingham's publication record runs to over twenty peer-reviewed papers since 1995, alongside dozens of conference presentations. Five stand out for their influence on how near-peer BLS teaching is understood and practised across UK Higher Education Institutions:

Basic life support training for health care students ↗

Perkins GD, Hulme J, Shore HR, Bion JF. Resuscitation. 1999;41(1):19–23.

Why it matters: The founding paper. It documents the original University of Birmingham programme handing BLS teaching to trained undergraduate peers — the empirical starting point for the near-peer model that RMD sites now run nationally.

Peer-led resuscitation training for healthcare students: a randomised controlled study ↗

Perkins GD, Hulme J, Bion JF. Intensive Care Med. 2002;28(6):698–700.

Why it matters: The randomised controlled trial that gave the model its evidence base — showing peer-led BLS teaching performs at least as well as tutor-led teaching. This is the result that made handing BLS instruction to students defensible, not just convenient.

Peer-led training and assessment in basic life support for healthcare students: synthesis of literature review and fifteen years practical experience ↗

Harvey PR, Higenbottam CV, Owen A, Hulme J, Bion JF. Resuscitation. 2012;83(7):894–899.

Why it matters: A fifteen-year synthesis of running the programme at scale — the most complete account of how a near-peer BLS scheme actually operates over time. It's the reference point other UK HEIs draw on when setting up or evaluating their own near-peer courses.

Mandatory cardiopulmonary resuscitation competencies for undergraduate healthcare students in Europe: A European Resuscitation Council guidance note ↗

Baldi E, Savastano S, Contri E, Lockey A, Conaghan P, Hulme J, et al. Eur J Anaesthesiol. 2020;37(10):839–841.

Why it matters: A policy-level guidance note co-authored with the European Resuscitation Council, mandating CPR competencies for undergraduate healthcare students across Europe. It marks the model's evidence base moving from a single UK institution to shaping continental curriculum policy.

Skill decay following Basic Life Support training: a systematic review protocol ↗

Stanley B, Burton T, Percival H, Beesley E, Coffin N, Hulme J, Owen A, Alderman J. BMJ Open. 2021;11(12):e051959.

Why it matters: Current, methodologically rigorous work tackling the field's open question — how quickly BLS skills decay after training, and what that means for refresher scheduling. It keeps RMD active at the research frontier rather than resting on its founding studies.
