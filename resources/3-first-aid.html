This is the beginning of the Choking session
The following slides will guide you through the management of choking in an adult and a child
RCUK Choking algorithm adult – structured approach to any adult who is choking
Key first step is to recognise they may be choking - commonly associated with eating. The induvial may start to cough, gag or place their hand to their throat.
Encourage coughing if they can’t effectively cough to clear the obstruction or are tiring then call for help 999
1st intervention: Back blows
Learn person forwards and deliver up to 5 blows between their shoulder blades, using the heel of your hand.
Check after each blow to see if obstruction has cleared

If back blows are ineffective, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts ( see next slide)


2nd intervention : Abdominal thrusts
Instructions as per bullet points
Repeat up to 5 times checking after each attempt to see if the obstruction has cleared

If the person is still choking after 5 abdominal thrusts alternate between 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the obstruction is cleared.
If the person becomes unconscious carefully lower them to the ground, call for help - start CPR
The same initial approach as an adult who is choking – recognition of potential obstruction is key – often associated with eating
Encourage coughing – if ineffective or person tiring then call 999 and shout for help.

All Paediatrics receive 5 back blows first –
for infants they can be place face down on the responder's lap.

For a child it is the same technique as adult: Learn the child forwards and deliver up to 5 blows between their shoulder blades, using the heel of your hand.
After each back blow check to see if the obstruction has cleared.
For infants – under 1 years of age
Perform up to 5 chest thrusts – checking to see if obstruction has been relieved .

If the obstruction remains after 5 chest thrusts , alternate between 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts until the obstruction has cleared or the person becomes unconscious.
Once unconscious place the infant on the floor, call 999 deliver 5 rescue breaths and begin CPR.

In summary – key principles in the management of choking which we have covered are:

Early recognition of an obstruction is key
Calling for help early – 999 and help nearby
Encourage the person to cough – are the coughs effective?
Perform 5 back blows and move onto abdominal thrusts if the back blows weren’t effective – alternate between back blows and abdominal thrusts until obstruction is cleared or patient becomes unconscious – then you would start CPR
