Grey areas and common questions

  • Poor head tilt chin lift + inadequate rescue breaths
  • No pinch of nose during breaths
  • Candidate pausing CPR when assistant offers them help
  • Not checking for response/breathing before or after recovery position
  • A few candidates are unaware they are expected to do the full run-through
  • Staring at examiner during shock

Grey areas: hard fail points

FAIL
  • No danger check (evidence of a check is enough, no need to name hazards)
  • No response check (physical and verbal)
  • Never calls 999, or calls after a noticeable delay
  • Never asks for the AED, or an uncomfortably long delay in asking
  • No nose pinch during breaths
  • Breaths delivered with too much force
  • Hands off-centre on the chest
  • Compression rate or depth consistently wrong
  • Pad on the wrong side of the chest
  • Any pad contact during shock delivery
  • Turns the AED off instead of pressing shock (if it delays the shock)

Grey areas: not a fail

NOT A FAIL
  • Moves the manikin before starting
  • Does not shout in both ears
  • Does not say "not breathing / cardiac arrest" on the 999 call
  • Loses count while calling 999 or asking for the AED (must then complete a clean 30:2)
  • Breathing check slightly short of 10 seconds
  • Pads swapped, but each on the correct side
  • Pad falls off because of poor pad quality
  • Touches a pad after the AED says "apply pads firmly"
  • Briefly touches or kneels on the wire, once
  • Plugs the wire in before the pads are on
  • Hovers a finger over the shock button
  • Presses shock early but safely, recognises it, then shocks once charged
  • Briefly looks down to find the shock button

Grey areas: use your judgement

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT
  • Head tilt chin lift: fail only if considerable neck compression
  • Breathing check: very short or clearly ineffective is a fail
  • Compressions: 15 to 18 seconds per 30 (clicker off), over 50% of sets correct
  • Pause before resuming CPR after a shock: fail if uncomfortably long
  • Recoil: fail only if compressions look ineffective
  • Rescue breaths: over 50% effective passes, but the threshold to fail is low
  • Slow to return to compressions after breaths
  • Pad near the shoulder: tip over the shoulder is wrong, above the clavicle is fine
  • Switching the AED on late: fail if uncomfortably long
  • Position during shock: fail only if genuinely unsafe

When in doubt, discuss with the assessing committee before deciding

Reasonable Adjustment Pathway (RAP) students

  • Students who are unable to complete a standard BLS assessment for medical or other reason
  • Must be discussed with Student Faculty
  • Examples
  • Unable to do chest compressions
  • Manikin on table etc
Discussions around opening gambit, explanation of phone/tablet/laptop use (and context RE mistaken QCPR), discussion around verdict delivery and avoiding delaying verdict or giving too much feedback.

SUMMARY

  • Arrive on time
  • Re-familiarise with grey areas before assessment night
  • Avoid having document open during assessments (or WhatsApp messages mid-assessment)
  • Standardised approach
  • Feel empowered to fail borderline/poor performance
  • Student/Senior Faculty will support your decision making
  • Document fail reasons well on assessment form
  • Raise recurrent issues. Repeated issues are fed back to instructors
  • This feedback has been instrumental in improving candidate performance
