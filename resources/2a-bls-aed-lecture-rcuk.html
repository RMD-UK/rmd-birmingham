Why is basic life support with AED so important? Highlight that as per RCUK guidelines, BLS includes the use of an AED
Cardiac arrest can happen anytime, anywhere to anyone: Most cardiac arrests, are in adults (98%), cardiac arrest in children is relatively rare however you may be surprised to know that one third (33%) of all arrests were aged 15-64 years.

Fewer than 10% survive an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: according to the latest statistics which translates into 30,000 attempted resuscitations by NHS ambulance services

Currently only 30-40% of out-of-hospital cardiac arrests have bystander CPR- We want the public will feel able to start CPR should they witness a cardiac arrest or come across someone who has collapsed and shows no signs of life.

CPR and use of the AED can triple the chances of survival. Effective CPR and use of AED until the emergency medical service arrive can make all the difference

AEDs can be used by anyone – no medical training required
What is a cardiac arrest?
When the heart suddenly becomes unable to pump oxygen around the body due, this may include when the heart stops beating completely.
The person may be breathing abnormally, or not at all – in some rare cases especially high level sports the person may appear to be breathing normally – key is that they are unresponsive still!

Sometimes it get confused with a heart attack.
A heart attack is a caused by a blockage in the blood vessels that supply blood and oxygen to the heart, causing damage to the heart.
Symptoms : pale, sweaty chest pain, radiating to back jaw/ arm. – call 999 immediately – sit the person down and wait for the ambulance.
Increasing survival rates isn’t just about CPR – multiple factors can influence the chance of someone surviving a cardiac arrest

The first link in the chain is the immediate recognition of cardiac arrest and calling for help.
The second is the prompt initiation of CPR and performing defibrillation as soon as possible.
The third is optimal post-resuscitation care.
Because we are ambitious, the fourth link in the chain is survival from cardiac arrest and restoration of quality of life.
Like any chain, it is only as strong as its weakest link. If one stage is weak, the chances of successful resuscitation outcomes are compromised.

CHECK CALL CPR

The key steps when someone has collapsed are: CHECK – CALL - CPR

Recognising ( CHECK ) they need help is vital, even if you are unsure if they need CPR or not, if unresponsive, then CALL – 999 and for help close by – and if required begin CPR as soon as possible . As mentioned earlier the sooner an induvial in cardiac arrest receives CPR the better the chances of survival are.

CHECK – CALL

When calling 999
Listen to the call taker and ask the questions – they are using a computer screen they need to follow
They will tell you what to do
Know you location or use what three words (THIS IS NOT ENDORSED BY ALL AMBULANCE SERVICES, I THINK IT’S OKAY TO MENTION)

Call 999 before checking for breathing – if you are unsure if they are breathing normally or not the call handler will help you.
If you know how to assess breathing do this while calling 999 so you are ready to answer the question when asked
The call handler will help to assess whether the patient is breathing or not
They will also instruct on how to give CPR – do not delay – start CPR immediately when breathing is assessed even if that’s at the same time as calling

If you have assessed the breathing and the patient requires CPR- do not wait until call handler instructs you – commence CPR as soon as possible.

If you are trained and feel comfortable you can deliver two rescue breaths – do not let it distract from high-quality CPR. ..


If you know where the nearest AED is, send someone to collect it or wait until the call handler identifies the closest one – you may need a code from the call handler to unlock the AED cabinet.
The call handler will help you to check for breathing
First, you need to open the airway
As you open the airway -you are looking for normal breathing
It is sometimes difficult to identify if the person is unresponsive with abnormal breathing. They may have ‘agonal breathing’
If able to do so , can assess breathing whilst call is connecting to 999

If you are unsure start chest compressions
