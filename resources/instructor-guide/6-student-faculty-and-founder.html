Team Teachers

Alessandro · Tess · Issy · Tegan

Team Teachers focuses mostly on instructors and assessors. The team recruits new and returning instructors and assessors over the summer, then helps organise Instructor's Weekend in October. Through the year they organise teaching rooms and roles for exam nights, sort out on-the-night issues, and run socials for instructors and assessors (previous years: Country Girl, Circo, in town, and post-Christmas pizza nights).

Team Admin

Stella · Ellie · Holly · Georgia

Admin handles everything BLS-student-related: liaising with administration leads across Medicine, Dentistry, Nursing, Physiotherapy and Pharmacy to allocate students to courses, managing absences, answering queries via the BLS email account, coordinating RAPs, and planning examination nights. After each course, Admin also issues students their European Resuscitation Council certificate via CoSY.

A message from our founder, Prof Jon Hulme

RMD: Resuscitation. Mentorship. Development was established in 1995 by three of us who were medical undergraduates at Birmingham. We wanted to replace didactic, lecture-based teaching of basic life support (BLS) with a unique, interdisciplinary, near-peer programme. All three of us were active members of the University Lifesaving Club — I was its chair. We taught, practised and assessed life support regularly during the week, and it was that shared experience and enthusiasm with our friends that let us set up RMD and make it work.

We recruited well-performing students and fostered their interest, teaching them to become life support instructors and assessors. As a group pioneering this style of scheme across large-scale Higher Education Institutions, we undertook and published original research demonstrating the scheme's benefits and success.

Since then we've grown and innovated. The course is accredited by the European Resuscitation Council (ERC) and endorsed by the Faculty of Intensive Care Medicine. The British Heart Foundation and Health Education England fund our community outreach programme, MiLE (Midlands Life Support Education), bringing essential life support skills to secondary school pupils across the West Midlands.

Now overseen by a faculty of clinical and non-clinical staff — still including one of the founders — and run by a student faculty, our course is one of the largest in Europe. Cited by the General Medical Council (GMC) as an innovative teaching method, we've empowered many to become instructors, assessors and leaders. RMD has since been set up at other UK Higher Education Institutions, where the same sense of community, enthusiasm and shared passion has kept it the success it is.

I've been lucky to be involved in many things that have interested me as a student, a doctor in training, and then as a consultant. But setting up RMD, and having it remain a constant part of my career, is one of the things I'm most proud of. Sharing what we do together keeps building and evolving. Those of us who are older members of the team find that while we teach and guide, we learn too — because of the enthusiasm, professionalism, fun and zeal you bring to the organisation. It is you who makes RMD what it has always been, and what it can become: thank you for all that you bring to it.
