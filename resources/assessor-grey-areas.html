Grey Areas & the Borderline Candidate

How we will use this section

  • Each scenario is a ‘what if’ you might see on assessment night
  • We want to decide if it is FAIL / NOT A FAIL
  • We'll click to reveal the ruling
  • Discuss any disagreement as a group before moving on

Exam Prep

Exam Prep

Candidate moves the manikin before starting.

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail point. Best practice is for the examiner to place the manikin centrally so it can be approached from either side. Only flag to committee if there's a genuine attitude problem.

Approach

Approach — Danger Check

Candidate doesn't check for danger.

FAIL

No danger check = FAIL. They don't need to name specific hazards (global/local danger etc.) — just show evidence of a check.

Approach — Response Check

Candidate doesn't check for a response.

FAIL

No response check = FAIL. Both physical and verbal checks are expected. Not shouting in both ears is NOT a fail.

Approach — Head Tilt Chin Lift

Candidate's lower hand presses on the soft tissue under the chin.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

FAIL only if there's a considerable amount of neck compression — not just slightly incorrect positioning. Pulling up by the nose is technically wrong but not itself a fail point (only really possible to do on a manikin).

999 Call

999 Call

Candidate never calls / asks for an ambulance.

FAIL

No 999 / ambulance call = FAIL.

999 Call

Candidate calls, but only after a noticeable delay.

FAIL

Inappropriate delay in asking for or making the 999 call after the breathing check = FAIL.

999 Call

Candidate doesn't explicitly say “not breathing / in cardiac arrest” on the call.

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail — the call handler will prompt for this information through structured questioning.

Approach — Breathing Check

Candidate's breathing check is a little short of 10 seconds.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

Slightly short of 10s = NOT a fail. If it's very short or clearly ineffective, that IS a fail.

Chest Compressions

Compressions — Rate

Candidate's compression rate/depth is inconsistent across sets.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

Target: 15–18 seconds to complete each set of 30 (examine with the clicker OFF). Aim for over 50% of sets at the correct rate and depth. One-off wrong count (27–33 range) is NOT a problem — but consistently wrong = FAIL.

Compressions — Counting Mid-Cycle

Candidate loses count while calling 999 or asking a helper for the AED.

NOT A FAIL

The exact number during that cycle doesn't matter. They must complete an uninterrupted 30:2 cycle afterwards — give them the chance to complete three clean 30:2 sets if the first isn't perfect.

Compressions — Hand Position

Candidate's hands sit on the top half of the sternum, or the heel of the hand sits below it.

FAIL

Hand position must be in the centre of the chest. Off-centre in either direction = FAIL (ineffective or dangerous).

Compressions — Resuming After Shock

Candidate pauses noticeably before resuming CPR after a shock.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

They should resume immediately, without needing to be prompted. An uncomfortably long wait = FAIL.

Compressions — Recoil

Candidate doesn't allow the chest to fully recoil between compressions.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

Full recoil should be allowed between compressions. FAIL only if compressions look genuinely ineffective as a result.

Rescue Breaths

Rescue Breaths — Effectiveness

Some of the candidate's rescue breaths don't visibly inflate the chest.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

A majority (over 50%) effective = pass. If breaths are borderline, the threshold to fail is low.

Rescue Breaths — Nose Pinch

Candidate doesn't pinch the nose during breaths.

FAIL

No nose pinch = FAIL.

Rescue Breaths — Force

Candidate delivers breaths with too much force.

FAIL

Excessive force makes breaths ineffective = FAIL.

Rescue Breaths — Transition

Candidate is slow to get back into position for compressions after breaths.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

They must move efficiently back to chest compressions after rescue breaths.

AED — Asking & Pad Placement

AED — Forgetting to Ask

Candidate never asks a helper for the AED / defibrillator.

FAIL

Completely forgetting causes significant delay to shock delivery = FAIL. Even just an uncomfortably long delay in remembering to ask = FAIL.

AED — Pad Position

Pad sits close to the shoulder.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

The tip of the pad shouldn't sit over the shoulder, but sitting above the clavicle is fine.

AED — Pad Inversion

Candidate swaps the two pads around, but each is still on the correct side of the chest.

NOT A FAIL

Not a problem if positions are simply swapped. FAIL only if a pad ends up on the wrong side of the chest (left upper / right lower).

AED — Poor Adhesion

A pad falls off due to poor pad quality, not candidate error.

NOT A FAIL

If the candidate made a good initial effort to apply the pads, tell them to “continue as if the pads stayed on.” If they stick the pad back on themselves that's fine too, provided there's no excessive handling.

AED — Touching Pads

Candidate touches a pad after the AED prompts “apply pads firmly.”

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail. However, any pad contact during shock delivery itself = FAIL.

AED — Wire & Shock Delivery

AED — Wire Contact

Candidate briefly touches or kneels on the AED wire once.

NOT A FAIL

Not a problem in isolation. Repeated contact interferes with the AED's analysis and becomes an issue. FAIL if the wire ends up trapped under a pad (poor contact).

AED — Plugging In Before Pads

Candidate plugs the wire in before the pads are on the chest.

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail — the AED won't start analysing until the pads are actually on.

AED — Switching On Late

Candidate has pads on and plugged in, then takes a while to turn the AED on.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

FAIL only if there's an uncomfortably long delay switching it on.

AED — Position During Shock

Candidate leans over the patient, or otherwise looks unsafe, when delivering the shock.

BORDERLINE / USE JUDGEMENT

FAIL if it looks genuinely unsafe. If only an arm is partially over the patient and the position otherwise looks safe, that's NOT a fail.

AED — Hovering Over the Button

Candidate hovers a finger over the shock button before it's time.

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail, unless it contributes to an unsafe shock.

AED — Pressing Early But Safely

Candidate deliberately presses the shock button before the AED is fully charged, in a controlled and safe manner, then recognises this and delivers the shock safely once charged.

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail, as long as it's recognised and the shock is still delivered safely and promptly.

AED — Looking Down to Press

Candidate looks down briefly to locate the shock button.

NOT A FAIL

Not a fail, as long as it's only a minimal break in visual contact with the patient and surroundings.

AED — Turning It Off Instead

Candidate turns the defibrillator off instead of pressing the shock button.

FAIL

FAIL, as long as it causes a delay in defibrillation.

ANY QUESTIONS?

In summary...

  • Most “grey areas” have a clear rule once you know where the line sits
  • Danger check, response check, 999 call, nose pinch, hand position, and pad contact during shock are hard fail points
  • Minor imperfections in an otherwise safe, effective attempt are usually NOT a fail
  • When in doubt, discuss with the assessing committee before deciding
